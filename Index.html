1. Definition of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some microorganisms use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into organic compounds, mainly carbohydrates, with oxygen released as a by-product.

Overall Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Importance of Photosynthesis

  • Converts light energy into chemical energy.
  • Produces organic compounds used as food.
  • Releases oxygen into the atmosphere.
  • Provides energy-rich carbon compounds for organisms.
  • Forms the foundation of most terrestrial food chains.
  • Removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
  • Contributes to the global carbon cycle.

2. Chloroplast and the Site of Photosynthesis

In higher plants, photosynthesis occurs mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells.

Important Chloroplast Structures

  • Outer membrane: The outer boundary of the chloroplast.
  • Inner membrane: Forms the internal boundary of the chloroplast.
  • Stroma: Fluid-filled region where the Calvin cycle occurs.
  • Thylakoids: Flattened membrane sacs containing photosynthetic pigments and electron transport components.
  • Granum: A stack of thylakoids.
  • Stroma lamellae: Membranes connecting grana.

3. Photosynthetic Pigments

Photosynthetic pigments absorb light energy used during photosynthesis.

Chlorophyll a

  • The primary photosynthetic pigment.
  • Directly participates in the light reactions.
  • Its reaction centres are associated with P680 and P700.

Chlorophyll b

  • An accessory pigment.
  • Broadens the range of light wavelengths that can be absorbed.
  • Transfers absorbed energy to chlorophyll a.

Carotenoids

  • Include carotenes and xanthophylls.
  • Absorb additional wavelengths of light.
  • Help protect chloroplasts from excessive light.

4. Light-Dependent Reactions

The light-dependent reactions occur mainly on the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. They convert light energy into chemical energy stored in ATP and NADPH.

Main Events

  1. Light is absorbed by photosynthetic pigments.
  2. Electrons become excited.
  3. Water undergoes photolysis.
  4. Electrons pass through an electron transport chain.
  5. A proton gradient is established.
  6. ATP is produced through chemiosmosis.
  7. NADP⁺ is reduced to NADPH.
  8. Oxygen is released from water splitting.

Basic Light Reaction Pathway

LIGHT ↓ PHOTOSYSTEM II (P680) ↓ Electron Transport Chain ↓ PHOTOSYSTEM I (P700) ↓ NADP⁺ → NADPH Water ↓ Photolysis ↓ H⁺ + e⁻ + O₂ Proton gradient ↓ ATP Synthase ↓ ATP

Photolysis of Water

2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂

The electrons replace electrons lost by chlorophyll at Photosystem II, while oxygen is released as a by-product.

5. Photosystems

Photosystem II — PSII

  • Reaction centre: P680.
  • Functions early in the linear electron pathway.
  • Receives replacement electrons from water.
  • Contributes to oxygen production.
  • Helps establish the proton gradient used for ATP synthesis.

Photosystem I — PSI

  • Reaction centre: P700.
  • Re-energizes electrons.
  • Provides electrons used in the reduction of NADP⁺.
  • Contributes to NADPH production.
Exam point: Although it is called Photosystem II, PSII functions before PSI in the normal linear electron pathway.

6. Photophosphorylation

Photophosphorylation is the formation of ATP using light energy during photosynthesis.

Linear Photophosphorylation

H₂O ↓ PSII ↓ Electron Transport Chain ↓ PSI ↓ NADP⁺ → NADPH Produces: ATP + NADPH + O₂

Cyclic Photophosphorylation

In cyclic electron flow, electrons from PSI return through electron carriers to PSI instead of reducing NADP⁺.

┌───────────────┐ │ ↓ PSI → Electron Carriers ↑ │ └───────────────┘ ↓ ATP No O₂ produced No NADPH produced
Cyclic electron flow mainly increases ATP production and does not involve water splitting at PSII.

7. Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of the chloroplast. It uses ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions to incorporate carbon dioxide into organic molecules.

Three Main Stages

Stage 1 — Carbon Fixation

CO₂ combines with the five-carbon molecule RuBP. The enzyme RuBisCO catalyses the reaction.

The unstable six-carbon compound breaks down into two molecules of 3-PGA.

Stage 2 — Reduction

3-PGA is converted into triose phosphate (G3P) using ATP and NADPH.

Stage 3 — Regeneration of RuBP

Most G3P remains in the cycle and is used to regenerate RuBP. This allows the cycle to continue.

CO₂ ↓ RuBP ↓ [RuBisCO] ↓ 3-PGA ↓ ATP + NADPH ↓ G3P ↙ ↘ Carbohydrates RuBP ↑ └── Regeneration
The Calvin cycle does not directly use light. However, it depends on ATP and NADPH generated by the light-dependent reactions.

8. C3 Photosynthesis

C3 photosynthesis is the most common pathway of carbon fixation. It is called C3 because the first stable product of CO₂ fixation is the three-carbon compound 3-PGA.

Pathway

CO₂ ↓ RuBP (5-carbon) ↓ RuBisCO ↓ 3-PGA (3-carbon) ↓ Reduction ↓ G3P ↓ Carbohydrates

Examples

Many plants use the C3 pathway, including wheat, rice, beans and many temperate plants.

Characteristics

  • Initial CO₂ fixation is catalysed by RuBisCO.
  • First stable product is 3-PGA.
  • Carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle occur in the same cells.
  • Photorespiration can become significant under hot, dry conditions.

9. C4 Photosynthesis

C4 photosynthesis is an adaptation that reduces photorespiration by concentrating CO₂ around RuBisCO.

Initial Carbon Fixation

In mesophyll cells, CO₂ is initially fixed by PEP carboxylase to form a four-carbon compound.

MESOPHYLL CELL CO₂ ↓ PEP ↓ PEP Carboxylase ↓ Oxaloacetate ↓ Malate / other C4 acids ↓ Transport to bundle sheath ↓ BUNDLE-SHEATH CELL C4 acid ↓ CO₂ released ↓ Calvin Cycle ↓ G3P ↓ Carbohydrates

Examples

  • Maize
  • Sugarcane
  • Sorghum

Advantages

  • Reduces photorespiration.
  • Works well at high temperatures.
  • Efficient under high light intensity.
  • Maintains relatively high photosynthetic rates in hot environments.

10. CAM Photosynthesis

CAM stands for Crassulacean Acid Metabolism. It is an adaptation found in many plants living in dry environments.

Night

  • Stomata open.
  • CO₂ enters the plant.
  • CO₂ is fixed by PEP carboxylase.
  • Organic acids such as malate accumulate in vacuoles.

Day

  • Stomata are generally closed.
  • Organic acids are broken down.
  • CO₂ is released inside the leaf.
  • The Calvin cycle uses the released CO₂.
CAM PHOTOSYNTHESIS NIGHT Stomata OPEN ↓ CO₂ ↓ PEP Carboxylase ↓ Organic acids ↓ Stored as malate ↓ DAY Stomata CLOSED ↓ Malate broken down ↓ CO₂ released ↓ Calvin Cycle ↓ Carbohydrates

Importance

CAM conserves water by allowing gas exchange mainly at night, when temperatures are lower and humidity is often higher.

Examples

  • Cacti
  • Pineapple
  • Many succulents
