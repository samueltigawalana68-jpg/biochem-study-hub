🧬 Carbohydrates

Definition

Carbohydrates are organic compounds made mainly of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. They are an important source of energy and also have structural and biological roles in living organisms.

Main Classes of Carbohydrates

1. Monosaccharides

These are single sugar units. Examples include glucose, fructose, and galactose.

2. Disaccharides

These contain two monosaccharides joined together. Examples include sucrose, lactose, and maltose.

3. Polysaccharides

These are long chains of monosaccharides. Examples include starch, glycogen, and cellulose.

Functions of Carbohydrates

  • Provide energy for cells.
  • Store energy as glycogen in animals.
  • Store energy as starch in plants.
  • Provide structural components such as cellulose in plants.
  • Participate in cell recognition and communication.

Important Example — Glucose

Glucose is a major fuel used by cells. It can be broken down through glycolysis and other metabolic pathways to produce energy.
